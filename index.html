    What the terms mean

    10 terms

    No jargon left unexplained. These are the words used on this page, in plain English.

    Local SEO
    So you show up when somebody searches your service plus a town — “plumber St. Catharines”, “dentist near me”. It means your town and service written into the page titles and copy, a complete Google Business Profile, opening hours and service area as structured data, and a sitemap submitted to Google. It is the base Google needs to be able to rank you; nobody honest can promise position one.
    Advanced local SEO
    Everything in local SEO, plus one optimised page per service and per town instead of a single generic page, internal links between them, schema markup, and pages built around the searches that actually bring local customers. It is the difference between appearing for your own name and appearing for the thing you sell.
    Google Business Profile
    The free Google listing that appears in Maps and on the right of search results, with your hours, photos, reviews and phone number. For most local businesses it is the single most valuable free channel there is. I set it up or fix it, link it to your site, and hand you the owner login.
    Sitemap and Search Console
    A sitemap is the machine-readable list of your pages; Search Console is Google’s free dashboard showing what people searched to find you, which pages Google indexed and whether anything broke. Both are set up in your name, so you can look at real search data whenever you want instead of taking my word for it.
    Analytics with conversion tracking
    Visits are not the number that matters; enquiries are. A form submission, a click-to-call, a booking request or a direction tap is a conversion, and tracking means you can see how many happened and which page they came from, instead of guessing whether the site is doing anything.
    Mobile-first
    Designed on a phone first, then widened for a laptop. Around seven in ten local searches happen on a phone, usually from someone standing outside your door, so tapping a number or sending a form has to work one-handed before anything else does.
    Revision round
    One pass of changes you send as a single list. Starter includes one, Business two, Growth three. Sending changes one at a time over weeks is what turns a short build into a long one, so the round keeps the timeline honest. Extra rounds are $75 CAD flat.
    Priority slot
    Your build and your requests go to the front of the queue. On Growth it means starting sooner after the deposit and being answered before other work; on the Complete care plan it means same-day replies.
    Enquiry dashboard
    One simple screen showing how many enquiries arrived this week and last, where they came from and what they asked for. No marketing-speak, no graphs you cannot read, just the number a business owner actually wants.
    Pass-through cost
    Money paid to somebody else — the domain registrar, a host, an ad platform, a booking tool. It is billed at what it costs, paid in your name, and is never marked up as my fee.

    Questions owners ask about photography

    9 questions
    Who actually takes the photographs?

    The sessions are shot by my wife, a professional photographer, and I handle the website and the file preparation. It is not something we do on the side: the photos arrive already sized, cropped and named for the pages and the posts they will sit on, instead of a folder of files you have to sort out yourself.

    Do I own the photos, and can I use them anywhere?

    You get full usage rights to every edited image: your website, social media, print, menus, flyers, directories and paid ads, with no time limit and no extra licence fee. Most photographers charge separately for commercial use; here it is included. The one thing I keep is the right to show a few images in my own portfolio, and you can say no to even that. What is not sold is exclusive ownership: I will never license your photos to a competitor as their own, but I do not hand over the copyright.

    Do I get the RAW files?

    No. RAW files are the unedited camera originals, and they are not delivered — that is standard practice. You get the fully edited high-resolution images plus the web-sized and vertical versions. If you want more images edited after delivery, extra editing is quoted per image rather than sold as unedited files.

    What if it rains, or I need to move the session?

    For outdoor sessions we either move to another day at no charge or shoot indoors, whichever you prefer, and interior sessions are not affected by weather at all. Rescheduling is free with 24 hours or more notice. Inside 24 hours the session moves once at no charge; a second last-minute move is treated as a new booking.

    How long do you keep my gallery, and can I order more later?

    Your delivered gallery stays online for 12 months so you can download it again whenever you need, and I keep an archive copy after that. Re-opening an old gallery is free. Extra editing beyond what the session includes is quoted per image, and a follow-up session is always available at the same rate as the first one.

    Is there a travel fee?

    None anywhere in the Niagara Region: St. Catharines, Niagara Falls, Welland, Niagara-on-the-Lake, Thorold, Grimsby, Fort Erie and the surrounding towns are all included. Anywhere further out in Southern Ontario, including Hamilton, Burlington and the GTA, is a flat $75 CAD travel fee agreed before you book.

    What should I do before the shoot?

    We start with a short planning call: what you sell, who your customers are, which rooms and jobs matter most, and a shot list we build together so nothing important is missed on the day. On the day, tidy the space the way you would for a customer, tell your team what is happening so nobody hides from the camera, and put out whatever you want photographed — dishes, products, finished work, uniforms.

    Will you photograph my staff, and what about releases?

    Team portraits and candid working shots are part of the Half Day and Content Day, and anyone can step out of the shoot if they would rather not be photographed. No model releases are needed for photos you use on your own website and social media. If you ever want to run an image with an identifiable employee in a paid campaign, we sort a simple release first.
